Sandalwood face pack benefits come down to three things: it calms inflamed, acne-prone skin, gently fades tan and dullness, and cools down skin that is stressed from heat or sun. It works slowly, though, not as an overnight fix.

Chandan, as sandalwood is called in most Indian households, has been sitting in bathroom cabinets for generations. It shows up in wedding rituals, temple offerings, and grandmother's skincare drawer, usually for the same reason: it feels cooling and calming the moment it touches your skin.

But like most traditional ingredients, sandalwood gets both overhyped and underused. This guide covers what it actually does, how to use it properly, and where people go wrong.

## Is There Any Real Science Behind Sandalwood for Skin?

Sandalwood powder comes from the heartwood of the sandalwood tree, and its natural oils and compounds are what give it a skincare reputation that has lasted centuries, not just marketing.

The tree's heartwood contains santalol, an aromatic compound that carries most of the anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial activity researchers have studied. This is the same compound responsible for sandalwood's calming scent, which is also why it shows up in perfumery and incense, not just face packs. Ayurvedic texts have documented sandalwood, or chandana, as a cooling agent for centuries, and modern dermatology broadly agrees on its anti-inflammatory and mild antibacterial effects, even though it is not positioned as a replacement for prescription acne treatment.

That said, "natural" does not mean "risk-free" or "instant." Sandalwood works gradually, and its effects are supportive rather than curative. Treat it as a calming, brightening addition to a routine, not a stand-alone fix for serious skin concerns.

## What Are the Real Benefits of a Sandalwood Face Pack?

### Calms redness and acne-prone skin

Sandalwood carries natural anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties. Applied on the skin, it can reduce the redness around active breakouts and calm the irritation that comes with inflamed acne. It will not clear cystic acne on its own, but as a supporting step in a routine, many people find it genuinely soothing.

### Helps fade tan gradually

One of sandalwood's most searched benefits is tan removal. It has mild anti-tanning properties that help lift dull, sun-darkened surface cells when used consistently. It is not instant, and it works best alongside sun protection. If tan is your main concern, our detailed guide on [how to remove tan at home](/blog/how-to-remove-tan-at-home) covers the full picture, including how turmeric-based packs compare.

### Naturally cooling for heat-stressed skin

Ask anyone who has grown up in India during peak summer, and they will tell you sandalwood paste feels like relief on hot, tired skin. This cooling sensation is not just tradition talking. It genuinely helps calm skin that feels warm, tight, or irritated after long hours outdoors.

### Controls oil without over-drying

Sandalwood absorbs some surface oil, which makes it useful for oily and combination skin. Unlike stronger clays, it tends to be gentler, so it suits people whose skin gets irritated by harsher oil-control ingredients.

### Evens out dullness and gives a soft glow

Regular use can leave skin looking brighter and less patchy over a few weeks. This is less about instant "glow" and more about consistently removing dead surface cells and calming inflammation, both of which make skin look more even.

## How Do You Use Sandalwood on Your Face the Right Way?

The method matters more than the ingredient itself. Here is a simple, repeatable process.

### Step 1: Cleanse your face first

Start with a clean, dry face using a gentle cleanser. Sandalwood applied over makeup, sunscreen, or sweat will not work as well, since it needs direct contact with skin.

### Step 2: Mix a smooth, thin paste

Take about a teaspoon of sandalwood powder and mix it with rose water until you get a paste roughly the thickness of yogurt. Rose water is the classic pairing here because it hydrates while the sandalwood calms and cleans. We cover this pairing in more depth in our post on [rose water for face](/blog/rose-water-for-face).

### Step 3: Apply an even, thin layer

Spread the paste evenly across your face and neck, avoiding the eye area and lips. A very thick layer takes too long to dry and can feel heavy, while a layer that is too thin dries out fast and does little.

### Step 4: Let it sit for 10 to 15 minutes

Leave the pack on until it feels slightly dry but not fully cracked. This is usually around the 10 to 15 minute mark, depending on the weather and your skin type.

### Step 5: Rinse with lukewarm water

Wash it off using lukewarm water and gentle circular motions. Avoid hot water, which can strip moisture that sandalwood is trying to preserve.

### Step 6: Moisturise right after

Even gentle ingredients like sandalwood can leave skin slightly dry. A light, non-greasy moisturiser locks in hydration and keeps your skin balanced.

## How Often Should You Use a Sandalwood Face Pack?

Two to three times a week works well for most skin types. Oily and acne-prone skin can go slightly more often, closer to alternate days, while dry and sensitive skin should stick to once or twice a week. Overusing any face pack, sandalwood included, can leave skin feeling tight and stripped. ## Sandalwood for Different Skin Types: What Actually Works?

Not every skin type should use sandalwood the same way. Here is a quick, honest breakdown.

### Oily and acne-prone skin

Sandalwood mixed with rose water works well here. It calms breakouts and controls mild oil without stripping skin completely. Avoid mixing it with milk or cream-based liquids, since these can feel heavy and may contribute to clogged pores.

### Dry skin

Sandalwood alone can feel drying on already dry skin. Mixing it with a touch of raw milk or a moisturising base helps balance this out, and using it once a week instead of three times is a safer starting point.

### Combination skin

A single all-over layer usually works fine, since sandalwood is gentle enough not to over-dry the drier areas while still calming the oilier T-zone. If you want a more targeted routine for combination skin, apply a slightly thicker layer only on the oily zones.

### Sensitive skin

Sandalwood is generally considered gentle, but "generally gentle" is not the same as "safe for everyone." Always patch test on your inner arm 24 hours before your first face application, and stop immediately if you notice itching, burning, or redness.

## Sandalwood vs Turmeric vs Multani Mitti: How Do They Compare?

People often confuse these three because they show up together in a lot of DIY recipes. Here is how they actually differ.

Sandalwood is best known for calming inflammation, gently fading tan, and cooling heat-stressed skin. It is milder than the other two, which makes it a good everyday option for sensitive or combination skin.

Turmeric is stronger on the anti-tanning and brightening front, thanks to its active compound curcumin, but it can temporarily stain the skin yellow and needs to be used in smaller amounts. Our [turmeric face pack benefits](/blog/turmeric-face-pack-benefits) post covers safe usage in detail.

Multani mitti is the strongest of the three for pure oil control and deep pore cleansing, since it is a clay built to absorb. Many people do not need to pick just one. Rotating between sandalwood for calming days and multani mitti for oilier days is a common, sensible approach.

## What to Do If Your Sandalwood Pack Is Not Working?

A few quick fixes for the most common frustrations people report.

- **Skin feels tight after rinsing.** You likely left the pack on too long or used too little liquid. Shorten the time to 10 minutes and moisturise immediately after rinsing.
- **No visible change after a few uses.** Tan fading and dullness improvement take several weeks of consistent use, not a handful of applications. Stick with two to three uses a week for at least a month before judging results.
- **Mild breakouts after starting sandalwood.** This is uncommon but can happen if the paste is too thick or left on too long, clogging pores. Thin the paste and shorten the time on skin.
- **Powder feels gritty or does not dissolve well.** Lower-quality or coarsely ground sandalwood powder can feel gritty. Look for a finer, well-sieved powder, or reduce the quantity and mix longer to smooth it out.

## Common Mistakes People Make with Sandalwood

A few honest warnings, because sandalwood gets oversold online just as often as it gets underused.

- Using it daily. Even a gentle ingredient like sandalwood can dry out skin with daily use. Two to three times a week is enough for most people.
- Expecting overnight tan removal. Sandalwood fades tan gradually over weeks, not days. Anyone claiming instant results with a single application is exaggerating.
- Skipping the patch test. Sandalwood is gentle for most, but allergic reactions do happen. A quick patch test on the inner arm avoids an unpleasant surprise on your face.
- Mixing with harsh acids. Combining raw sandalwood powder with lemon juice or other strong acids can irritate skin, especially before sun exposure. Milder liquids like rose water are safer.
- Letting the pack dry completely. A fully dried, cracked pack pulls more moisture out of the skin than needed. Rinse it off while it is still slightly damp.
- Forgetting to moisturise afterward. Skipping moisturiser after any face pack, including sandalwood, can leave skin feeling tight and undo some of the calming benefit.
